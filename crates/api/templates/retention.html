Data Retention & Erasure

Last updated: June 1, 2026

{# 1. Overview #}

1. Overview

This policy describes how tagsmaxxing (“we,” “our,” or “us”) retains and deletes customer data. It covers the full data lifecycle: from upload to deletion, including the cryptographic erasure (“crypto-shredding”) process that ensures data is irrecoverable after deletion.

{# 2. Data Retention During Active Use #}

2. Data Retention During Active Use

While your account is active, we retain:

  • Uploaded files: Stored in the blob store (encrypted at rest) for as long as the associated document exists;
  • Document metadata: Tags, summaries, titles, user notes, and document structure retained for the life of the document;
  • Search embeddings: Vector embeddings retained for the life of the associated chunks;
  • Usage records: Token usage and API call records retained for billing and quota enforcement;
  • Account information: Email, hashed password, workspace details, and team membership retained for the life of the account;
  • Audit logs: Security-relevant events (decrypt access, key operations) retained for the life of the tenant.

You may delete individual documents at any time through the application. Deleting a document removes its metadata, tags, chunks, and embeddings. The associated files are queued for permanent deletion from the blob store.

{# 3. Account Deletion #}

3. Account Deletion

When you request deletion of your account (through account settings), we initiate a multi-stage deletion process:

  1. Immediate suspension (T+0): Your account is deactivated. All access tokens and sessions are revoked. No further API calls or logins are accepted. Your data is no longer accessible through the Service.
  2. Grace period (T+0 to T+7 days): Your data is retained in encrypted form but inaccessible. During this period, you may contact support to reverse the deletion if the request was made in error.
  3. Crypto-shredding (T+7 days): The per-tenant Data Encryption Key (DEK) is permanently deleted. All file content, metadata, and embeddings encrypted with that DEK become irrecoverable — no ciphertext can be decrypted without the key.
  4. Blob store cleanup (T+7 to T+30 days): Encrypted blobs are garbage-collected from the object store. Database rows for the tenant are purged. Backups containing the tenant’s data age out according to the backup retention schedule (maximum 30 days).

After 30 days, no trace of the tenant’s data remains in the live system or backups.

{# 4. Cryptographic Shredding #}

4. Cryptographic Shredding (Crypto-Shredding)

tagsmaxxing uses a two-tier encryption key hierarchy to enable secure, verifiable data erasure:

Key Hierarchy

  • Key Encryption Key (KEK): One per deployment cluster. Stored in a hardware security module (HSM) or secure keystore. The KEK never leaves the secure boundary. It is used exclusively to wrap (encrypt) and unwrap (decrypt) Data Encryption Keys.
  • Data Encryption Key (DEK): One per tenant. Generated on tenant creation using a cryptographically secure random number generator (CSPRNG). Stored encrypted (wrapped by the KEK). Used for all tenant data encryption: file content (AES-256-GCM), sensitive metadata, and encryption-at-rest for search indexes.

When crypto-shredding is triggered:

  1. The tenant’s DEK is permanently deleted from the database;
  2. Any in-memory copies of the DEK are zeroed;
  3. Since all tenant data was encrypted with that DEK, and the DEK itself was encrypted with the KEK, deleting the DEK renders the ciphertext irrecoverable — even if an attacker obtains the ciphertext and the KEK, there is no DEK to decrypt;
  4. A deletion audit event is recorded (tenant_id, timestamp) as proof of erasure;
  5. Backup retention ensures that any backup containing the DEK ages out within 30 days.

Crypto-shredding is the primary deletion mechanism because it is:

  • Fast: Deleting a single key is near-instantaneous, regardless of how much data was encrypted;
  • Verifiable: The absence of the DEK record is auditable proof of erasure;
  • Secure: Even if encrypted blobs linger in storage or backups, they are mathematically irrecoverable without the DEK;
  • Compliant: Meets GDPR Article 17 (Right to Erasure) and similar data protection requirements.
{# 5. Backup Retention #}

5. Backup Retention

Database backups are retained according to the following schedule:

  • Daily backups: Retained for 7 days;
  • Weekly backups: Retained for 30 days;
  • Monthly backups: Retained for 90 days.

Backups are encrypted at rest using the same encryption infrastructure as live data. After crypto-shredding, any backup containing the deleted tenant’s DEK will have that key rendered useless within the retention window — and the backup itself will expire and be purged. The maximum time for complete purge of tenant data from all backups is 30 days (weekly backup window).

{# 6. Export Before Deletion #}

6. Data Export Before Deletion

Before deleting your account, you may export your data using the built-in export feature. The export includes:

  • All original files;
  • Document metadata (titles, summaries, tags, user notes);
  • Chunk and embedding data in JSONL format;
  • A manifest with schema version and export metadata.

The export format is fully round-trippable — you can import it into another tagsmaxxing instance. See the FAQ for instructions.

{# 7. Legal Holds #}

7. Legal Holds

In rare circumstances, we may be required by law to preserve data beyond the normal retention period (e.g., in response to a valid legal process). If a legal hold is placed on your account, we will notify you unless prohibited by law. Data subject to a legal hold is exempt from the standard deletion schedule until the hold is lifted.
